=== Fraction of reads mapping to the fungal genome -- 2 WT vs 2 KO ===
Mean: WT = 67.8%   KO = 18.9%
t-test between groups: t = -4.89, P = 0.128
Correlation between fungal fraction and group (KO=0/WT=1): r = 0.96

ftm cluster: mean |logFC| = 6.09, median |r| with the fungal fraction = 1.00
Other genes of comparable |logFC| (4.4-8.7, n = 164): median |r| = 0.96
Wilcoxon P = 3.52e-04  (EXPLORATORY -- see the caveats below)
  -> the cluster tracks the fungal read fraction MORE closely than the background

Read this comparison as descriptive, not as a hypothesis test. Three
things make the P value far smaller than the evidence warrants:
  1. The fungal read fraction is not a measurement of biomass. It is the
     share of a mixed library that maps to the fungal genome, which also
     moves with RNA yield, rRNA depletion and bacterial transcriptional
     activity.
  2. Wilcoxon treats every gene as an independent observation. The eight
     ftm genes sit in one co-regulated cluster and are anything but
     independent, so the effective sample size is far below n = 8.
  3. The background set is chosen to match the OBSERVED |logFC| of the
     ftm genes, and |logFC| is itself a function of the group difference
     that the fungal fraction tracks. The matching is therefore partly
     circular.

With 4 libraries, the honest conclusion is that a contribution of
differing fungal biomass CANNOT BE EXCLUDED from RNA-seq alone. Settling
it needs qRT-PCR against a fungal housekeeping gene, or qPCR of fungal
ITS against bacterial 16S, or dry weight.

=== ftm cluster AFTER adjusting for the fungal fraction ===
mean logFC = 1.34   (unadjusted: -6.09)
Cluster-level test after adjustment: P = 0.645

INTERPRETATION. The adjusted model must not be read as evidence that the
effect disappears. The covariate correlates with the group at r = 0.96, so the
two are nearly collinear and the group effect is barely estimable. More
importantly, if bacillibactin restricts fungal growth then the lower fungal
fraction in the wild-type co-culture is part of the biological effect, i.e. a
mediator rather than a confounder, and adjusting for a mediator removes the
very quantity the experiment set out to measure. This limitation is resolved
experimentally -- by qRT-PCR normalised to a fungal housekeeping gene, by
direct biomass measurement, and by LC-MS quantification of verruculogen --
not statistically.
